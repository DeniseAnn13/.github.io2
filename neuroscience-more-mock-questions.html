More Mock Exam Questions

Here are 12 additional short-answer prompts. Try answering each in 2–4 sentences or with a labeled diagram before opening the answer points. These are practice questions, not a prediction of the exact exam.

Suggested practice: Set a timer for 20–25 minutes. Answer all questions first, then check your responses. Give yourself credit for accurate explanations in your own words.

1. A patient has damage to a brain region and loses a particular ability. What can this observation suggest, and why is it not conclusive by itself?

Check key points

The damaged region or pathway may be necessary for the ability. But lesions can affect connected tissue or fibers, functions rely on networks, and compensation or lesion extent can affect results.

2. Which technique would best show detailed brain anatomy without X-ray radiation, and which technique can track a radioactive metabolic tracer?

Check key points

MRI provides detailed structural anatomy without ionizing radiation. PET uses a radioactive tracer to track a selected metabolic or biochemical process.

3. Explain why an fMRI signal is an indirect measure of neural activity.

Check key points

fMRI measures changes in blood oxygenation and flow (the BOLD signal) associated with neural activity, rather than recording action potentials directly. The blood response is also slower than neuronal signaling.

4. Trace the direction of sensory and motor information through the PNS.

Check key points

Sensory (afferent) information travels from receptors toward the CNS. Motor (efferent) commands travel from the CNS to effectors such as muscles and glands.

5. In a spinal nerve pathway, which root carries sensory fibers and which carries motor fibers?

Check key points

The dorsal root carries sensory/afferent fibers and contains the dorsal root ganglion. The ventral root carries motor/efferent fibers. They join to form a mixed spinal nerve.

6. Name the glial cells that form myelin in the CNS and PNS, and state one difference between them.

Check key points

Oligodendrocytes form CNS myelin; Schwann cells form PNS myelin. One oligodendrocyte can myelinate segments on multiple axons, while a myelinating Schwann cell wraps one segment of one axon.

7. What structures form the core physical barrier of the BBB, and what is one function of the barrier?

Check key points

Brain capillary endothelial cells joined by tight junctions form the core barrier, supported by pericytes, basement membrane, and astrocyte end-feet. It helps stabilize the brain’s chemical environment and selectively controls what moves from blood into brain tissue.

8. Why does the Na⁺/K⁺ pump matter for resting potential, and what is its transport ratio?

Check key points

It maintains the sodium and potassium gradients that support the resting potential and repeated signaling. Each cycle moves 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in, using ATP.

9. What causes the rising and falling phases of a typical action potential?

Check key points

Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open and Na⁺ enters, causing depolarization. Na⁺ channels then inactivate while voltage-gated K⁺ channels open; K⁺ leaves and repolarizes the membrane.

10. What is the difference between absolute and relative refractory periods?

Check key points

During the absolute refractory period, another action potential cannot be initiated. During the relative refractory period, another can occur, but usually requires a stronger-than-normal stimulus.

11. Describe the sequence from an action potential arriving at a synaptic terminal to neurotransmitter binding.

Check key points
An action potential depolarizes the terminal, voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open, Ca²⁺ enters, and Ca²⁺ triggers vesicle fusion and neurotransmitter release. The transmitter crosses the cleft and binds receptors on the target cell.

12. A neuron receives several inputs from different synapses at nearly the same time. What type of summation is this? How would repeated signals from one synapse be classified?

Check key points

Inputs from different synapses combine through spatial summation. Repeated inputs from one synapse arriving close together combine through temporal summation.
