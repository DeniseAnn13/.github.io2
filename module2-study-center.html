Rapid summary

Sensation

  • Receptors transduce physical or chemical energy into neural activity.
  • Thalamic relays commonly send sensory information to primary cortical areas.
  • Perception combines receptor signals with cortical organization and experience.

Movement

  • Cortex plans and executes voluntary movement.
  • The cerebellum improves accuracy, timing, and learning.
  • Basal ganglia help select and initiate actions.

Regulation

  • Homeostasis maintains targets; allostasis anticipates changing demands.
  • Temperature, thirst, and appetite use sensors, hormones, and behavior.
  • Obesity involves genetics, psychology, environment, and metabolic adaptation.

Sleep

  • Light is a major circadian zeitgeber.
  • Sleep medications affect GABA, melatonin, orexin, histamine, or adenosine signaling.
  • Sleep theories emphasize restoration and adaptation.

Exam strategy

  1. Define the term.
  2. Describe the mechanism or pathway.
  3. Explain the receptors or brain structures involved.
  4. Compare it with a similar process.
  5. Give one clinical or behavioral example.
